# Midterm Questions

These are the questions explicitly stated or strongly signaled in the lecture notes. Lecture 01 was an introduction class and had no explicit exam questions.

## Lecture 02 - 2026-04-28

- Define the differences between the words **sex** and **gender**.

- Why is it important to distinguish between **sex** and **gender** / why is it important to study sex and gender?

- Why was the **gender binary** the norm? Why did people think it was common or natural in society?

## Lecture 03 - 2026-05-05

- What is **gender identity**?

- Do you think gender identity is important for you to have? Why is it important?

- If you were the government or had authority to make legal change, how would you strengthen gender identity in your country or community?

- As an individual or citizen, how would you strengthen your own gender identity?

- Do **gender** and **sex** influence a person's destiny?

- Is your destiny in your own hands, or in other people's hands, such as society's hands or the religious sector's hands?

- Why are **gender roles** important?

- How does gender identity influence a country's development?

- How can societies strengthen and support gender identity, both through individuals and through the government sector?

- What is **gender nonconforming**?

## Lecture 04 - 2026-05-12

- What were **Olympe de Gouges** and **Mary Wollstonecraft** trying to propose, and what were their achievements?

## Lecture 05 - 2026-05-14

- Compare and contrast **Charlotte Perkins Gilman** and **Emma Goldman**.

- Do you agree or disagree with **Charlotte Perkins Gilman's** idea that communities should provide shared kitchens, professional childcare, and communal domestic services to free women from housework and childcare?

- Do you agree or disagree that when women gain the **right to vote**, they can be liberated?

- Analyze what issues inside European or American women's organizations slowed their progress, especially divisions over race, class, and which women should receive voting rights.

## Lecture 06 - 2026-05-19

- Talk about the **strengths and weaknesses of goal-oriented institutions and multiple-goal institutions**.

- When women gain rights, which is more important: **more opportunity/equality** or **more protective laws for women**?

## Lecture 07 - 2026-05-21

- Discuss whether the government and second-wave feminists were sincere in recreating a new image of women. Are they trying to make women look better, or are both sides forcing women to change? Who is worse, who is better, or are they the same?

- Compare feminist and conservative understandings of **private** and **public** matters.

- Explain the second-wave feminist focus on **sexual liberation** and **bodily autonomy**, especially how feminists challenged social expectations about sexuality.

- In the internal debate about **pornography**, who are you siding with: liberal feminists or radical feminists? Who is more reasonable to you, and why?

## Lecture 08 - 2026-05-26

- How do Western feminists view themselves, and how do women from less developed countries view Western feminists?

- Criticize the conflict between **cultural rights** and **women's rights**. Which one is more important to you?

- Based on the 1980 conference, the characteristics of developing countries, and the urgent-issues slide: which side is more reasonable to you, Western feminists or women from less developed countries? Why?

- List issues that are considered urgent for women in less developed countries, list the characteristics of less developed countries, and explain why those issues are urgent.

- Why is **poverty** an urgent issue?

- Why is **healthcare** an urgent issue?

- Make a list of benefits you can gain in your life without having children.

- Are women's issues in less developed countries caused mainly by **patriarchy**, or mainly by the issue of being **underdeveloped**?
