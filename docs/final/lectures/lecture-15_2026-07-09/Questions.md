# Questions - Lecture 15, 2026-07-09

## Salem Witch Trials & Puritan Society

- Possible essay focus: **Salem Witch Trials as a case study of gender-based violence and Puritan social control**.

- The essay should include:

    - Puritan beliefs in strict morality, religious discipline, fear of sin, fear of the Devil, and the need to purify the community.

    - The role of **community surveillance**, gossip, church attendance, and public reputation in controlling behavior.

    - How religion justified violence by framing punishment as protection from evil.

    - Why women who were poor, outspoken, independent, culturally different, or socially disliked were easy targets.

    - The first accusations against **Tituba**, **Sarah Good**, and **Sarah Osborne**.

    - The roles of **Betty Parris**, **Abigail Williams**, and **William Griggs** in turning strange behavior into witchcraft panic.

    - The trial process: accusation, examination, confession or denial, imprisonment, execution, and public punishment.

    - The significance of the trials: mass hysteria, weak legal protection, violation of individual rights, and punishment of people who did not fit the norm.

## Witch Hunts And Confession Process

- Possible essay focus: **witch hunts as systems of fear, conformity, and forced confession**.

- The essay should include:

    - How fear spread after the first accusations.

    - How pressure to name witches turned vulnerable women into targets.

    - How forced confession could be used for survival but also reinforced belief in witchcraft.

    - Tituba's confession as a key example because it suggested that other witches existed in the community.

    - The cycle of fear: accusation, pressure, confession, more fear, and more accusations.

    - The role of weak evidence, rumors, dreams, hallucinations, and community pressure.

    - Why the desire to end the witch hunt quickly actually increased the number of accusations.

## Witchcraft And Intersectionality

- Possible essay focus: **how overlapping inequalities shaped witchcraft accusations**.

- The essay should include:

    - A definition of **intersectionality** as overlapping systems of oppression.

    - **Tituba** as an intersectional example shaped by race, enslavement, gender, religion, culture, class, and lack of legal protection.

    - **Sarah Good** as an example of poverty, homelessness, class prejudice, motherhood expectations, and anger being turned into suspicion.

    - **Martha Corey** as an example of a woman punished for speech, criticism, religious confidence, and independence.

    - **Bridget Bishop** as an example of economic independence, colorful dress, public sociability, sexual reputation, and gossip being treated as deviance.

    - **Sarah Osborne** as an example of age, church absence, and vulnerability making a woman suspicious.

    - How accusations were systemic rather than random because they followed existing patterns of gender, class, race, religion, and authority.

## Origins And Demonization Of Witchcraft

- Possible essay focus: **how non-Puritan healing and spiritual practices were redefined as evil**.

- The essay should include:

    - Tituba's herbs, healing knowledge, songs, stories, blessings, and ancestor-spirit beliefs.

    - How Puritan fear turned unfamiliar cultural practices into signs of witchcraft.

    - How the line between healing and witchcraft depended on who had the authority to define the practice.

    - How demonization created fear, and fear made persecution easier to justify.
