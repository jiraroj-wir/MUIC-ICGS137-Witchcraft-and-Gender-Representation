## Notes

### Patriarchy And Primary Power

- **Patriarchy** is a social system where men hold primary power and dominate society.

- In a patriarchal system, men usually predominate in:

    - **Political leadership**.

    - **Moral authority**.

    - **Social privilege**.

    - **Control of property**.

- Primary power includes the power to:

    - **Rule**.

    - Control **politics**.

    - Control **morality**.

    - Hold **privilege** over others.

    - Define and control **property**.

- Property is important because property is something a person is understood to own and control.

- When men control the definition and ownership of property, patriarchal society can turn women, children, or other people into things treated as men's property.

- This helps explain why women have often been described as the property of fathers, husbands, or male guardians.

### Power, Feminist Criticism, And Bias

- One feminist claim is that men who see power often try to **use** or **abuse** that power over others.

- Another feminist claim is that women who hold power are more likely to use it to **accommodate**, **reduce conflict**, and **support peace**.

- This contrast is useful for understanding feminist criticism of patriarchy, but it should not be treated as an absolute truth.

- Not all men use power abusively.

- Not all women use power peacefully.

- Some feminist arguments can become too extreme if they assume all men and all women behave in one fixed way.

- A strong essay should be able to explain the feminist critique while also recognizing where the critique may become biased.

### Hunting-Gathering Equality And The Agricultural Shift

- During the hunting and gathering period, men and women were presented as relatively equal because both contributed important labor to survival.

- Men's work:

    - Hunting large animals.

    - Taking physical risks.

    - Working intensely, but not necessarily every day.

- Women's work:

    - Gathering vegetation.

    - Hunting small animals.

    - Working continuously.

- The key point is that both forms of labor produced value for the group.

- Because both men and women contributed to the economy, they were understood as more equal.

- Women also had a voice in choosing leaders within some tribal contexts.

- Patriarchy became stronger after the **agricultural revolution**, when people settled down and society became more complex.

- Larger settled societies created new needs:

    - Discipline.

    - Guidance.

    - Administration.

    - Conflict control.

    - Cooperation between mixed groups.

- Trade, intermarriage, and contact between different tribes or ethnic groups created more diverse communities.

- Patriarchy is presented as one system that helped organize this complexity, even though it also created hierarchy and oppression.

### Gender Roles As A Product Of Patriarchy

- **Gender roles** are one product of patriarchal culture.

- Gender roles assign people expected routines and responsibilities.

- The benefit of gender roles is that they can create:

    - Predictability.

    - Social order.

    - Less confusion over who performs which tasks.

    - A shared direction for the community.

    - Completion of necessary work such as childcare and household labor.

- The harm of gender roles is that they can be **oppressive**, especially toward women.

- Gender roles can restrict people into fixed positions instead of allowing choice, talent, or fairness to decide responsibilities.

- A strong answer should not only say that gender roles are oppressive.

- It should also analyze why gender roles persist and what benefits society believes it gains from them.

### Products Of Patriarchal Culture

- Patriarchal culture produces several forms of gender inequality:

    - **Negative female portrayal**.

    - **Unequal pay**.

    - **Abuse**.

    - **Objectification**.

    - **Unrealistic beauty standards**.

    - **Rape culture**.

    - **Male-dominated industries**.

    - **Gender stereotypes**.

    - **Shaming language**.

- Beauty standards are a useful example of patriarchal control.

- Male-dominated culture can define what counts as beautiful or not beautiful.

- Women may then feel pressure to become the kind of woman that men or society label as attractive.

- Objectification connects to property:

    - If women are viewed as objects, they become easier to control.

    - If women are treated like property, violence and ownership become easier to justify.

### Ending Gender-Based Violence

- An essay on gender-based violence should address both **strategies** and **obstacles**.

- Possible obstacles include:

    - Culture.

    - Social beliefs.

    - Weak laws.

    - Weak enforcement.

    - Institutions that protect tradition over victims.

    - Systems that treat violence against women as a private family matter.

- Possible strategies include:

    - Improving education.

    - Reforming education to include gender equality and gender studies.

    - Public workshops and awareness programs.

    - Stronger laws against abuse.

    - Better enforcement of existing laws.

    - Changing cultural beliefs that normalize male control.

    - Increasing women's economic independence.

    - Protecting women's right to leave unsafe relationships.

- A strong answer should explain why gender-based violence is difficult to end, not only list solutions.

### Chimpanzees As An Icon Of Patriarchs

- Chimpanzees are used as an icon of patriarchal society because they are a male-dominated animal society.

- Male chimpanzees fight and use force to get what they want, including access to female chimpanzees.

- This example supports the feminist association between patriarchy, force, and male domination.

### India As A Patriarchal Country

- India is used as an example of a patriarchal country because men dominate women and many women have limited freedom.

- Women may be treated as a lower social class and experience violations of their rights.

- Examples of harm include:

    - Rape.

    - Murder.

    - Dowry-related pressure.

    - Burning.

    - Domestic violence.

    - Discrimination.

- A central belief discussed in this lecture is that women should marry early and live under men's protection.

- This belief becomes a root of other problems:

    - A woman may be judged as valuable only if she marries.

    - Marriage may require dowry.

    - Pressure to afford dowry may push women into unsafe labor or unsafe public spaces.

    - Women who refuse marriage may face violence or punishment from family.

    - Unmarried women may be treated as useless or socially undesirable.

- The point is not only that individual acts of violence happen.

- The deeper issue is that a cultural belief about marriage and female worth can produce many connected forms of suffering.

### Peru As A Patriarchal Country

- Peru is used as a patriarchal case because women experience high levels of abuse.

- Many jobs are reserved for men, making women more economically dependent.

- Economic dependence pushes women toward marriage or male protection.

- When women are made dependent on men, their social value is reduced and abuse becomes easier.

- Important statistics discussed:

    - About **52 percent** of Peruvian women have been slapped by a partner.

    - A larger share of women have experienced physical or mental abuse.

- Peru shows that patriarchy can be identified not only by male political control, but also by the level of violence and dependence women experience.

### The United States And Political Representation

- The United States is also described as patriarchal because men dominate political representation.

- A simple way to evaluate patriarchal power is to look at who holds government office.

- If most representatives, senators, or political leaders are men, the political system still reflects male dominance.

- This point can also apply to Thailand or other countries where political representatives are mostly men.

### How To Identify A Patriarchal Country

- A country may be considered patriarchal when:

    - Men dominate political, economic, or social power.

    - Women are abused or have their rights violated.

    - Women have limited freedom or limited access to resources.

    - Men hold most government positions.

    - Social norms push women into dependence on men.

- Patriarchy is not only about individual men.

- It is a social system that organizes power, law, property, morality, labor, and representation.

### Matriarchy

- **Matriarchy** is the opposite system discussed after patriarchy.

- Matriarchy is defined as a **mother-centered society** based on **maternal values**.

- In matriarchy, the female leader views herself less as a ruler and more as a mother who cares for children.

- The leader's role is to:

    - Care.

    - Protect.

    - Consider the best interests of the community.

    - Reduce conflict.

    - Maintain social harmony.

- The mother-centered idea does not necessarily mean women rule over everyone with force.

- It means mothers or maternal values stand at the center of culture.

### Maternal Values

- Maternal values include **equality**.

- Equality means people should have equal status and also contribute equally to society.

- Maternal values also include **consensus finding**.

- Consensus finding means everyone has a duty to find benefits for society.

- Benefits can include:

    - Food.

    - Products.

    - Valuable resources.

    - Diplomatic alliances.

    - Anything that gives the community an advantage.

- Maternal values also include **gift giving**.

- Gift giving means sharing what one has with others as generosity, not as a forced obligation.

- Gift giving creates social connection through kindness, appreciation, respect, and trust.

- This differs from patriarchal obligation, where exchange can feel forced or controlled.

- Maternal values also include **peace-building**.

- Peace-building means settling problems through accommodation, compromise, negotiation, and harmony instead of force.

### Keeper Of The Land And Distributor Of Food

- In matriarchy, women are described as **keepers of the land**.

- A keeper maintains the health of the environment and the well-being of the people.

- This differs from a patriarchal idea of men as **protectors of the land**.

- Protection can imply force, defense, and doing whatever is necessary to protect what is owned.

- Keeping emphasizes care, maintenance, and smooth functioning.

- Women are also described as **distributors of food**.

- A distributor shares resources and makes sure people can live well.

- This is connected to selflessness and community care.

- In contrast, patriarchy is associated with **control of food**:

    - What to produce.

    - How much to produce.

    - Who receives it.

- The contrast is between **distribution** and **control**.

### Essay Structure: Patriarchy, Matriarchy, And Development

- One major essay focus is to explain the characteristics of patriarchal and matriarchal societies.

- The essay should then compare the strengths and weaknesses of both systems.

- The final part should analyze what parts of patriarchy might help a country become developed, and whether matriarchy has features that could do the same.

- A developed country should not be understood only as a rich or industrial country.

- Development can include:

    - Infrastructure.

    - Recognition of rights.

    - Quality of law.

    - Quality of citizens.

    - Type of economy.

    - Political stability.

    - Social welfare.

    - Protection from violence.

- Patriarchal countries such as the United States, England, and many European countries are often considered developed, even though they remain patriarchal in many ways.

- A strong answer should ask which features of patriarchy may support development:

    - Organization.

    - Discipline.

    - Centralized authority.

    - Clear roles.

    - Control over production and property.

- The answer should also ask which features of matriarchy may support development:

    - Equality.

    - Care.

    - Consensus.

    - Peace-building.

    - Resource distribution.

    - Lower domestic violence.

    - Community well-being.

### Elephants As An Icon Of Matriarchy

- Elephants are used as an icon of matriarchy.

- In an elephant herd, an older female or older male can become leader if the elephant proves it can protect the herd.

- The key idea is that leadership is earned through ability, care, and protection.

- This contrasts with patriarchy, where power may be gained through force, domination, or inheritance.

- In matriarchal logic, a woman proves her worthiness to lead rather than taking power by force.

### Why Matriarchy Is Usually Small-Scale

- Existing matriarchal societies are usually small communities rather than large modern countries.

- One reason is population size.

- When a community has fewer people, problems can be more easily:

    - Compromised.

    - Negotiated.

    - Settled through consensus.

- When population grows larger, a society may rely more on force, law, hierarchy, and centralized control.

- This may explain why the modern world has matriarchal communities but not many fully matriarchal countries.

### Mosuo Women In China

- The Mosuo community near Tibet is an example of a matriarchal society.

- Important features:

    - Women are often heads of households.

    - Power and inheritance pass through the female line.

    - Women make business decisions.

    - Children belong to their mothers.

    - Women choose their own partners.

    - Men take care of animals and support women.

    - Men often stay with their mothers rather than living permanently with wives.

- Sexuality is treated differently from many patriarchal societies.

- Single women may choose partners freely, and sexuality is not framed mainly through virginity or shame.

- The arrangement where men stay with their mothers is presented as one reason domestic violence may be lower.

- If husbands and wives do not live together permanently, there are fewer everyday opportunities for husbands to dominate or abuse wives.

### Matriarchal Community In India

- A matriarchal community in India is used as a contrast to patriarchal India.

- Important features:

    - Women share the same rights as men, and sometimes more rights.

    - Women can divorce.

    - Women can have multiple husbands.

    - Women are heads of families.

    - Women own property.

    - Property passes to daughters.

    - Women own businesses, gardens, and plantations.

    - Women are educated.

    - After marriage, men move to live with the wife's family.

    - Men are expected to keep a low profile and not dominate the household.

- The right to divorce can reduce domestic violence.

- If a woman can leave an unhealthy or dangerous marriage, violence can be prevented before it becomes worse.

- Societies that deny women the right to divorce may trap women in violent relationships.

- Having multiple husbands is also a possible weakness of matriarchy.

- It can create questions about:

    - Paternity.

    - Family structure.

    - Whether the practice conflicts with widely accepted family values.

### Additional Matriarchal Case Studies

- **Minangkabau** communities in west Sumatra, Indonesia, show a matrilineal pattern:

    - Land passes through daughters.

    - Women hold important inheritance rights.

    - Men often take visible religious and political roles, so the society combines maternal property lines with male public authority.

- **Hopi** communities in northeastern Arizona show another form of mother-centered social organization:

    - Women are central to clan and household institutions.

    - Mothers exercise moral and political roles.

    - Women participate in political decision-making.

    - Men may still dominate some political and ceremonial systems, so matriarchy does not always mean women control every sphere.

- **Bribri** communities in Costa Rica and northern Panama show matrilineal land and ritual authority:

    - Land is handed down from mothers to children.

    - Women can prepare the sacred cacao drink for religious rituals.

    - Women's ritual role shows respect for maternal lineage and cultural continuity.

- These examples show that matriarchy is not one single model.

- Some communities center women through inheritance, some through household authority, some through ritual responsibility, and some through moral or political roles.

- A strong comparison should ask which parts of power are female-centered and which parts may still remain male-dominated.

### Strengths And Weaknesses Of Patriarchy

- Possible strengths of patriarchy:

    - Clear hierarchy.

    - Discipline.

    - Centralized authority.

    - Clear gender roles.

    - Efficient command in large populations.

    - Social stability when people accept assigned roles.

    - Control over property, labor, and production.

- Possible weaknesses of patriarchy:

    - Male dominance.

    - Gender inequality.

    - Objectification of women.

    - Domestic violence.

    - Rape culture.

    - Unequal pay.

    - Limited freedom for women.

    - Political underrepresentation of women.

    - Treating women and children as property.

- A strong answer should not romanticize patriarchy.

- It should explain why patriarchy can create order and development while also producing serious inequality and violence.

### Strengths And Weaknesses Of Matriarchy

- Possible strengths of matriarchy:

    - Equality.

    - Care and protection.

    - Consensus finding.

    - Gift giving.

    - Peace-building.

    - Lower domestic violence.

    - Women's property rights.

    - Women's right to divorce.

    - Female leadership based on ability and care.

    - Community-centered distribution of resources.

- Possible weaknesses of matriarchy:

    - It may work better in small communities than in large states.

    - Consensus can become difficult with large populations.

    - Maternal values may be idealized too much.

    - Claims that women naturally use power peacefully can become biased.

    - Multiple-husband systems may challenge common family structures and create paternity questions.

    - A system centered on women can still produce inequality if men are pushed into low status.

- A strong answer should evaluate matriarchy critically, not simply present it as the perfect opposite of patriarchy.
