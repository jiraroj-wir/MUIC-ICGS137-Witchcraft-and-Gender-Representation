## Origins And Demonization Of Witchcraft

- Possible essay focus: Explain how witchcraft began as practical healing, protection, luck, and spiritual support, then became defined as evil.

- Include:

    - Witchcraft helped people solve daily problems, cure symptoms, seek luck, protect themselves from spirits, and gain emotional relief.

    - Witchcraft appealed because it offered immediate help rather than asking people to wait for future reward or accept suffering.

    - Christianity condemned witchcraft because it challenged monotheism, recognized female divine power, and was accused of using Satanic power.

    - Demonization helped maintain church authority, reduce women's influence, and reshape public perception of witches.

    - Evil acts associated with witches included flying, invisibility, killing through magic, and shape-shifting.

## Witch Hunts And Confession Process

- Possible essay focus: Explain witch hunts as systems of fear, coercion, legal injustice, and social control.

- Include:

    - The process moved from accusation to examination, then confession or denial, then punishment.

    - Accused people often lacked lawyers and had little ability to defend themselves.

    - Torture, isolation, public humiliation, threats, hunger, and mental pressure were used to force confession.

    - Confession could be coerced, but denial could be treated as lying, so the accused faced a trap.

    - Some confessions could be strategic if confessing seemed like the only way to reduce pain, survive longer, or satisfy authorities.

    - Witchcraft became a **secret crime** or **impossible crime** because it was imagined as hidden, private, and difficult to prove.

    - The lack of direct evidence made accusation easier because people believed secret rituals could happen without witnesses.

## Witchcraft And Intersectionality

- Possible essay focus: Explain why witchcraft accusation was gendered but also shaped by age, class, marital status, reputation, and social protection.

- Include:

    - Women were targeted because women were seen as suspicious, morally weak, or dangerous when they had knowledge or independence.

    - Older women were vulnerable because their age, appearance, wisdom, and healing knowledge matched stereotypes of witches.

    - Poor women, widows, single women, and women without male protection were easier to accuse.

    - Women with scars, pets, unusual behavior, many marriages, or public mistakes could be treated as outside the norm.

    - Accusation worked as social control by punishing people who did not fit accepted gender expectations.

## Salem Witch Trials And Puritan Society

- Possible essay focus: Use the general witch-trial process to prepare for Salem as a case study of religion, fear, gender-based violence, and community control.

- Include:

    - Accusations could begin from fear, rumors, conflict, jealousy, or community tension.

    - Examination relied on pressure, leading questions, public suspicion, and weak evidence.

    - Accusers, interrogators, watchers, and searchers all helped build cases against the accused.

    - Watchers turned past behavior into evidence, while searchers turned ordinary objects into suspicious objects.

    - Witch trials reveal how religion and law could hide violence under the language of protecting the community.

    - A strong answer should connect witch trials to fear of the unknown, strict morality, social discipline, and punishment of people who did not fit the norm.
